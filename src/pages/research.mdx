# Research

<div className="lead">
How do independently-developed digital twins become an ecosystem that software agents can
actually reason about? That question was the subject of my Ph.D. thesis, and I keep pulling
on the thread.
</div>

I'm part of the [Pervasive Software Laboratory](https://pslab-unibo.github.io/) (PSLab) at the
Department of Computer Science and Engineering, University of Bologna.

## Digital Twin Ecosystems

Digital twins are usually built one at a time, for one system, by one team. The moment you want
two of them to cooperate, you discover that neither can describe itself to the other.

I work on hypermedia and Linked Data as the interoperability layer: **self-describing digital
twins** that publish their own affordances, so a client — human or agent — can discover what a
twin represents and what it can do at runtime rather than at integration time.

- <Cite title="Towards Linked Data for Ecosystems of Digital Twins" year /> — the modelling side of the problem.
- <Cite title="Hypermedia-based digital twin ecosystems" year /> — the full proposal, in *Future Generation Computer Systems*.
- <Cite title="HWoDT Framework" year /> — the toolchain that implements it.
- <Cite title="Digital Twin Continuum" year /> — pushing twins across the edge-cloud continuum.
- <Cite title="Distributing intelligent functionalities in the Internet of Things" year /> — where the intelligence should sit, between agents and twins.

## Engineering Multi-Agent Systems

The BDI model gives agents a vocabulary for beliefs, desires and intentions — but the frameworks
that implement it have real engineering gaps.

- **Concurrency** — <Cite title="Concurrency Model of BDI Programming Frameworks">why we should control it</Cite>, and <Cite title="On the External Concurrency of Current BDI Frameworks">what current frameworks actually do</Cite>.
- **Explainability** — <Cite title="explainability framework for engineering and understanding BDI agents">a multi-level framework</Cite> for engineering and understanding BDI agents, extended to <Cite title="Engineering Inter-agent Explainability">explanations between agents</Cite>.
- **Anticipatory reasoning** — <Cite title="Reflexive anticipatory reasoning by BDI agents" year />, and <Cite title="Anticipatory Thinking in Multi-Agent Contexts">what anticipation means in multi-agent contexts</Cite> (Best Paper, WESAAC 2025).
- **Languages** — [JaKtA](https://jakta-bdi.github.io/), a BDI agent DSL embedded in pure Kotlin: <Cite title="JaKtA: BDI Agent-Oriented Programming in Pure Kotlin">the language</Cite> and <Cite title="Blending BDI Agents with Object-Oriented and Functional Programming">how it blends with OO and functional code</Cite>.

## Web of Things & Hypermedia MAS

If the environment of an agent is the Web, then hypermedia controls are its affordances. I'm
interested in how agents discover and use them, and what it takes for people who aren't
programmers to configure such systems.

- <Cite title="Domain-Expert Configuration of Hypermedia Multi-Agent Systems" year /> — putting industrial hypermedia MAS in the hands of domain experts.
- <Cite title="Towards Framing the Agents" year /> — the Agents & Artifacts model, framed at the knowledge level.
- <Cite title="Towards Agents' Embodiment in Hypermedia Multi-agent Systems" year /> — what embodiment means in a hypermedia space.
- <Cite title="The Gap Between BDI Agents and Semantic Hypermedia" year /> — and what we can do about it.
